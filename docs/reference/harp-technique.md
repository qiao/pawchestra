# Harp technique: how a harpist sits at the harp and plays it

This file is the target of the pose and the motion of the deer harpist of the app. It describes
the size of the harp, the place of the harp at the player, the lean, the hands and their
registers, the arms, the hand shape, the pluck, the swing of a plucked string, the rest, and the
fingering of a harpist, with a source for each fact.

**Upkeep.** This file is current. A change of the pose, the motion, or the place of the harp
updates section 6 "Use in the app", section 7.10 for a change of the fingering, and each fact that
it finds wrong, in the same commit. Each new fact gets its source.

**Source marks.** Each fact names its source by a number of section 8. "(derived)" marks a value
that this file calculates from the values of its sources. A fact with no mark comes from the text
of the source.

## Terms

- **Soundbox.** The hollow back of the harp. It runs from the base up to the top of the harp, at
  the player. The strings start on its soundboard.
- **Column.** The pillar of the harp, at the far side from the player.
- **Neck.** The curved top bar from the top of the soundbox to the top of the column. The strings
  end in it.
- **Bass strings.** The long, low strings near the column. The treble strings are short and near
  the top of the soundbox, at the player.

## 1. The size of the harp

- A pedal harp stands about 1.8 m high, weighs about 36 kg, and has 47 strings, seven in each
  octave [7]. A concert grand of 47 strings goes from G7 down to C1 [21].
- The C strings are red, the F strings black or blue, and the other strings white [7].
- The lowest strings are wound with copper or steel wire, the middle and lower strings are gut,
  and the higher strings are nylon [7].
- A Lyon & Healy Style 23 is about 188 cm high, with a Sitka spruce soundboard of about 55 cm
  [19].
- On a concert harp, the D♭2 string is 97.5 cm long, D♭3 54.2 cm, D♭4 31 cm, and D♭5 17.6 cm [9].
  On a Salvi harp of 44 strings, the highest string is 0.071 m long and the lowest 1.475 m [20].
- In equal temperament with A4 at 440 Hz, the C1 string sounds at about 32.7 Hz, and the G7
  string at about 3136 Hz (derived).
- A lever harp of 26 strings (Dusty Strings Ravenna 26) is 35 inches (0.89 m) high at the back and
  39 inches (0.99 m) at the front, from C3 to G6 [6]. A leg, a stand, or a step-stool raises it to
  a comfortable height [6].
- A harp that is too short for the player can stand on a small wooden box [4]. Some smaller harps
  are played with no tilt back [4].

## 2. The place of the harp and the lean

- The harp rests on the right shoulder, as near the neck of the player as possible, and on the
  inner side of both knees [1]. It sits on the right shoulder or the collarbone, out of the hollow
  of the neck [2].
- The player pulls the harp back between the legs and sits up straight. The harp moves a little
  to the right, balances lightly on the right shoulder, and the knees hold a part of it [3].
- The player tilts the harp back to its point of balance, so that the shoulder does not hold all
  of its weight [3]. At the right balance, a shrug of the right shoulder moves the harp forward
  [2].
- Present-day harpists put the harp a little to the right of the middle line of the body [5].
- The harp is diagonal to the player. Thus neither arm crosses the middle line of the body [4].
- On a lever harp, the tilt back onto the right shoulder makes the strings about vertical [4]. On
  a pedal harp on the shoulder, the strings are oblique [1].
- No source gives the tilt in degrees. The tilt follows from the two contacts: the soundbox on the
  right shoulder and the base on the floor or a stand (derived).
- The player sits erect, neither too tense nor too relaxed [1], with the spine straight [2], and
  does not lean back in the upper register [1]. The head does not rest on the harp [1].

## 3. The hands and the registers

- The right hand plays the treble strings, and the left hand the bass strings [5]. The left hand
  comes to the strings from the left side of the harp, and the right hand from the right side
  (derived from [1, 5]: the harp rests on the right shoulder).
- The left arm, stretched straight out, touches the column with the finger tips, or wraps the
  lowest string with the hand. Then the harp is at the right place on the shoulder [2].
- With the arms stretched out, the hands come near the middle of the strings [4].
- The fingers play a little below the middle of the strings, and the thumb a little above it [1].
- Both hands are at almost the same level on the strings. The left thumb is a little lower than
  the right thumb, but higher than the fingers of the right hand [1].
- The correct seat height puts both hands at the middle of the strings in the middle register,
  with the forearms horizontal and the shoulders down [1].

## 4. The arms, the wrists, and the hand shape

- Each forearm is horizontal ("elbow up") [1]. In the upper register, the left elbow is a little
  lower [1].
- The elbows rise to a relaxed position, and the wrists go a little down [3].
- The wrists curve in and do not rest on the soundboard. The right wrist can touch it in the upper
  register [1].
- The hand curves, with a hollow in the palm. The fingers point down and play with the finger
  tips [1, 3].
- The thumbs stand upright, almost vertical, a little toward the fingers, with a big space between
  the thumb and the second finger [1]. The thumbs point up [3].
- A harpist plays with the thumb and the first three fingers, and seldom with the little finger
  [7].

## 5. The pluck and the rest

- After a pluck, each finger closes into the palm at once, and the thumb bends over the second
  finger [1]. The closed hand then rises, with the wrist curved in [1].
- When one hand plays alone, the other hand rests lightly on the edge of the soundboard, or gets
  ready for the next passage [1].
- In a rest from the playing position, the shoulders drop and the arms hang loose at the sides
  [25].

**The swing of the string.**

- At the release, the string has the form of a triangle, with its apex at the pluck point [9].
  The triangle splits into two waves that travel to the two ends, each with half of the
  displacement [22].
- A pluck excites the mode n of the string in proportion to 1/n², and a pluck at 1/n of the
  length gives no partial n [23]. A pluck at one third of the length puts about 68% of the energy
  into the first mode (derived from [9, 23]).
- A mezzo-forte pluck of the D♭2 string releases it at a displacement of about 4 mm on average,
  and from 0.5 mm to 7 mm at the extremes [9]. No source gives the displacement of other
  dynamics or of the treble strings.
- The first free swing is close to an ellipse. Its long axis lies 7° to 43° from the plane of the
  strings, so the string swings mostly across itself in that plane [9]. Before the release, the
  finger turns the string by 7° to 82° [9].
- The swing at a right angle to the soundboard gives its energy to the soundboard and decays
  fast. The swing parallel to the flat soundboard decays slowly [24].
- The first partial of the C♭3 string (123.6 Hz) decays with a damping factor of about 0.57 to
  0.68 [24]. As a factor for each second of the amplitude, this is a time constant of about 1.5 s
  and a T60 of about 10 s (derived). The source does not state the unit. No source gives the
  decay of the treble strings.
- A film camera with a shutter of 1/48 s sees more than one full swing of each string above about
  48 Hz, from about G1 up (derived). A lower string shows only a part of its swing in one frame.

## 6. Use in the app

The deer plays the harp at its place of `HARP_PLACE` of `src/models/instruments/harp.ts`, with the
hold `HANDS_HOLDS.harp` of `src/pose/hands.ts`.

The app follows these facts:

- **The size (section 1).** The deer plays a harp of 0.975 of the concert harp of the app
  (`SpeciesParams.harpScale`), 1.27 m high. Its base stands on the floor, so the pedals lie at the
  hooves. The crown and the neck near it stand lower than on a
  concert harp (`HARP.column`), so the muzzle of the deer stays at least 5.5 cm above the neck,
  as the head does not rest on the harp (section 2).
- **The place (section 2).** The base stands in front of the chest of the deer, a little to the
  left of the middle, and the top of the soundbox rests against the chest, in front of and below
  the right shoulder, near the place where a harpist rests it on the shoulder. The harp is
  diagonal to the deer, with the column at the front left (`HARP_PLACE.turn`).
- **The knees (section 2).** The harp stands between the knees. The left hoof rests to the left of
  the base, behind the foot of the soundbox, with the knee out and forward (`LEG_RESTS` of
  `src/pose/harp-feet.ts`). A hoof presses each pedal at its end (`HARP_PEDALS.foot`).
- **The trunk (section 2).** The spine stays erect (`HANDS_HOLDS.harp.lean`). A lean forward
  brings the long muzzle to the neck of the harp.
- **The registers (section 3).** The left hand plays the bass strings from the left side, and the
  right hand the treble strings from the right side (`faceL`, `faceR`).
- **The elbows (section 4).** The left elbow points out and down (`poleL`). The right elbow rises
  out and up (`poleR`), so that the right arm passes outside the top of the soundbox and reaches
  the strings from the right side. No arm goes through the plane of the strings.
- **The wrists (section 4).** The left palm faces the strings and down (`faceL`), so that the
  left wrist bends only a little, as the wrists of a harpist go a little down. A palm
  square to the strings bends the left wrist of the short arm far back, because the left forearm
  points into the strings. The right palm also faces a little down, and a little back to the deer
  (`faceR`), so that the right forearm twists at most 45°.
- **The right hand in the upper register (section 4).** The right wrist can come near the
  soundboard there. For its highest strings, near the right shoulder, the right palm moves down
  the strings and a little away from them (`highR`), so the right elbow does not fold and the
  forearm does not twist at the neck of the harp.
- **The arm between notes (section 7.8).** The hand moves to a new group on a smooth path, in the
  time of its length (`HAND_MOTION.placeSpeed` of `src/pose/hand-notes.ts`). The first group of a
  hand places that time before its first pluck, and a short move between two near notes starts
  at the pluck before, so each hand arrives before its note.
- **The pluck (sections 5, 7.3).** After its pluck, the thumb bends over the palm (`thumbClosed`),
  and fingers 2 to 4 close into the palm (`curlClosed`). The last pluck of a group pulls the palm
  away from the strings, and a pluck inside a group pulls it only a little (`HAND_MOTION` of
  `src/pose/hand-notes.ts`).
- **The fingering (section 7).** Section 7.10 gives the rules of the app.
- **The strike point (section 7.3).** Each hand plucks at 0.37 of the string from the soundboard
  (`PLUCK.share` of `src/models/instruments/harp.ts`), so the bass strings are plucked low. The
  strings fill the frame from near the base of the soundboard to the neck near the column, as on
  a concert harp (section 1).
- **The swing of the string (section 5).** Each note swings its string
  (`stringSwingsAt()` of `src/pose/harp-strings.ts`). The swing starts at its full size at the
  note start, and the velocity sets its size. The largest swing goes from 7 mm in the bass, the
  extreme of the measure, to 1.5 mm in the treble (`STRINGS.swing` of
  `src/models/instruments/harp.ts`), times the scale of the harp. The swing decays with a time
  constant from 3 s in the bass to 0.3 s in the treble (`STRING_MOTION.ringSec`), so the C♭3
  string gets the measured 1.5 s, and faster after the note end (`dampSec`). The triangle of the
  pluck rounds off to the arc of the first mode (`roundSec`). The long axis of the ellipse lies
  25° from the plane of the strings, the middle of the measure (`STRINGS.swingTilt`).
- **The look of the swing (section 5).** The scene draws the envelope of the swing, as a film
  camera with a long shutter sees it (`src/scene/harp-strings.ts`): a translucent band, brightest
  at its edges, where the string turns.

The app does not follow these facts:

- **The shoulder (section 2).** The top of the soundbox rests against the chest, about 6 cm below
  the right shoulder joint, and not on the shoulder. The chest of the deer stands about 17 cm in
  front of its shoulder joints, so with the soundbox on the shoulder, the strings lay 15 to 19 cm
  further forward, out of the reach of its arms (derived from the body of the deer). The chest
  touches the neck and the top of the soundbox: it goes up to 1.5 cm into the neck in 2 % of the
  play (`pnpm body-clip harpist`).
- **The tilt (section 2).** The harp stands upright (`HARP_PLACE.tilt`). The soundbox of the
  model leans toward the player, and a tilt back puts the left paw of the deer into its body.
- **The reach (section 3).** The arms of the deer are short, also at 1.5 times the arms of the
  source mesh (42 cm), so the base stands near the stool, and the left hand plays only up to D4. A harpist reaches the column with the stretched left arm,
  and the left hand plays up to about C5.
- **The level forearms (section 4).** The left forearm rises from the elbow to the wrist, because
  the left elbow hangs below the strings. The right forearm falls from the raised elbow to the
  wrist.
- **The level hands (section 3).** The left palm is higher on the strings than the right palm
  (`palmL`, `palmR`). The short left arm reaches the low pluck points of the bass strings only
  with the palm far above them, so the fingers of the left paw do not touch the bass strings at
  their pluck points. For its highest strings, near the chest, the left palm moves nearer to the
  strings, lower, and toward the column (`highL`).
- **The size of the swing (section 5).** The band shows the real swing times
  `STRING_SWING.strength`, and a denser blur (`STRING_SWING.density`), because a swing of a few
  millimeters on the harp of the deer does not show in a stage shot.
- **The decay of each axis (section 5).** One time constant decays the whole ellipse. A real
  string loses the swing at a right angle to the soundboard faster. No source gives the treble
  decay, so the treble time constant is an estimate.
- **The lowest strings (section 5).** The band shows the whole envelope also below G1, where a
  real film frame sees only a part of the swing.
- **The rest (section 5).** In the rest pose, the arms hang at the sides, and each hoof touches
  the outer side of its thigh (`restL`, `restR`). The elbows bend about 129° and point back, and
  each wrist bends about 11° from the line of the forearm. A harpist can also rest the hands on
  the thighs. The deep belly of the deer covers the tops of its thighs, and its knees lie about
  57 cm from the shoulders, out of the reach of the arms. The sources give the rest of one hand
  on the edge of the soundboard only while the other hand plays. With each hand on a string, the
  rest pose looked the same as the play.
- **The fingers (section 7.1).** The deer paw is a fist: a free thumb and three fingers joined into
  the palm. Thus fingers 2 to 4 close as one group, after the last of their plucks in a group.

## 7. Fingering

### 7.1 The fingers and the hands

- A harpist plays with four fingers of each hand, and does not use the fifth finger [1, 12, 17].
  The numbers are 1 (the thumb), 2, 3, and 4 (the ring finger) [1, 14].
- Both thumbs are on top [11]. In each hand, the thumb plays the highest string of a position, and
  finger 4 the lowest [11, 14]. The largest gap of the hand is between the thumb and finger 2, so
  the widest interval of a chord goes there [11].
- The right hand cannot reach the lowest strings, because it reaches around the soundbox. Two
  hands do not play together below the bass staff [12]. Fast passages sound best in the middle
  octaves, and the highest octave has little room for the hands [12].
- Two hands can share one fast line [12]. An arpeggio goes from hand to hand in groups of at most
  four notes [11, 16].
- The strings of one pedal harp are about 13 mm apart in the middle and 15 mm in the bass [18].
  Thus a tenth (9 gaps) spans about 12 cm (derived).

### 7.2 Placing

- **Placing** puts the fingers onto their strings before they sound [1]. The harpist places at
  once after the pluck before, in the order of the play [1]. A sign of the score places a group of
  strings at once [8].
- In a chord, the four fingers go onto their strings at the same time [1]. In an exercise, the
  harpist places the next measure during the count of an empty measure [1].
- The fingers rest lightly on the strings [1]. The hand and the wrist keep the same angle when
  they place anew, and the line of knuckles 2, 3, and 4 sets that angle [1].
- When time separates two notes of a phrase, the next finger slides silently up along its string
  and back, so that the hand keeps its contact [1, 8].
- In an arpeggio at 120 beats per minute, the finger touches its string for the full 250 ms before
  the next note. In a chord at 60 beats per minute, the contact is a quarter of the 500 ms [9].

### 7.3 The pluck and the strike point

- The finger plays with its tip, and the thumb with its outer edge [1]. The pluck is a pull into
  the hand, with the full travel of the finger, because the strings are thick and tight [12].
- After the pluck, the finger closes into the palm, and the thumb bends over finger 2 [1]. The
  hand stays still, and only the fingers move. The strength comes from the arm and the back [1].
- The stick phase of a pluck lasts about 56 to 431 ms, and the slip phase about 1 to 6 ms [9].
  The finger mostly pulls the string out, and turns it by a mean of about 40° to 45° [9].
- Harpists pluck at about one third to two fifths of the string, from the soundboard to the neck.
  The fingers differ by less than 1% of the string length [9]. The fingers play a little below the
  middle, and the thumb a little above [1].
- **Près de la table** (p.d.l.t.) plays very near the soundboard, with a nasal sound like a guitar
  [8, 11]. The hand then spans at most an octave [11], and the wrists rest on the soundboard [1].

### 7.4 Scales, arpeggios, and chords

One fingering serves all scales, because the harp is diatonic [1]. The diagram shows one octave
up in one hand.

```text
 strings, low to high --->
 group A: 4  3  2  1    group B: 4  3  2  1
          (4 lowest, 1 highest)  ^
                                 '-- 4 passes under the thumb before the thumb
                                     plays. Then 3 and 2 go low and 1 high at once.
```

- **Scale up.** Before the thumb plays, finger 4 passes under it and goes low on its string. After
  the thumb plays, the hand pivots on finger 4 as the arm pulls back a little. Then fingers 3 and
  2 go low, and the thumb high, at the same time [1].
- **Scale down.** Before finger 4 plays, the thumb crosses over it and goes high on its string.
  After finger 4 plays, fingers 2, 3, and 4 go low at the same time. The elbow pushes the hand
  forward [1].
- **Groups.** One octave plays 4321 4321 up and 1234 1234 down [14]. In two or more octaves,
  finger 3 turns under after the tonic, so the octave plays in groups of 3 and 4 [14]. The
  arpeggios of seventh chords play groups of 4 only [14].
- **Hard figures.** One hand does not easily play five notes in one group. A figure that changes
  direction, such as an Alberti bass, is harder than a run [11].
- **Chords.** A chord has up to eight notes, at most four in each hand [11, 13]. One hand easily
  reaches a tenth, and spans at most an octave and a fourth [11, 13]. In a large stretch, the
  thumb need not stand up [1].
- **Rolled chords.** A chord with no mark rolls up a little, fast and even, and its last note falls
  on the beat [1, 11]. An octave or a chord of two notes does not roll [1].

### 7.5 Glissandos and repeated notes

- **Glissando.** The hand glides in the middle of the strings: up with finger 3, down with the
  thumb, the hand open and relaxed [8]. Down, each hand plays one note. Up, one hand can play
  thirds or fourths [11]. In a glissando of opposite directions, the hands cross [11].
- **Repeated notes.** A fast repeat is very hard, because a finger that goes back onto a ringing
  string muffles it [12]. A fast repeat uses two hands, or two strings tuned to one pitch, such as
  C sharp and D flat [11, 12].

### 7.6 Damping

- For silence, the harpist places the hands wide open on the strings [8]. A damp puts the flat of
  the hand firmly on the strings, with the fingers apart, from the lowest to the highest string of
  the group [8, 15].
- The left hand damps the bass after a passage while the right hand plays on [15]. The hand then
  leaves with a small outward turn of the wrist, and the finger tips leave last [1].
- **Étouffé** (sons étouffés) stops each note just after it sounds [8]. In skips, the left hand
  lies flat, plucks, and goes back flat at once. The flat hand stops the sound, and the finger that
  played does not. In steps up, the back of the last joint of the finger stops the string before
  [8].
- A staccato note needs a pluck and a damp, and a damp takes twice the time of a pluck. Thus fast
  passages cannot be staccato [11, 12].

### 7.7 The pedals

- Seven pedals set the seven note names, each to flat, natural, or sharp [7, 13]. The left foot
  moves D, C, and B, and the right foot E, F, G, and A [7, 12, 13].
- The heels stay on the floor as much as possible [1, 8]. The knee relaxes, so that the ball of the
  foot guides the pedal silently into its notch [1].
- A harpist moves 1 to 2 pedals per second in short bursts. Two pedals of one foot at the same
  time are difficult [8, 12]. The pedal moves follow the accents of the music [1].

### 7.8 The arm between notes

- In the left arm of three harpists, the elbow is raised about 48° to the front and bends about
  120°. The hand bends back about 26° from the forearm, which helps the fingers close [10].
- The shoulder stays almost still. The elbow, the wrist, and the hand move more, in that order. The
  path of the hand between two notes is smooth and bell-shaped [10].

### 7.9 Rules for a fingering program

No source gives a cost model of harp fingering. These rules follow from the facts above.

- **Hands.** The right hand plays down to about G2, the lowest line of the bass staff (derived
  from [12]). A hand plays at most four strings in one position, with the thumb on the highest
  string [11, 14].
- **Span.** A tenth is easy, and an octave and a fourth is the limit [11, 13]. The widest gap goes
  between the thumb and finger 2 [11]. At p.d.l.t., the limit is an octave [11].
- **Groups.** A run up plays 4321 and passes 4 under the thumb. A run down plays 1234 and crosses
  the thumb over 4 [1, 14]. Groups of 4 and 3 are normal, and a group of 5 is hard [11, 14].
- **Placing time.** A group is placed at the latest at the pluck of the last note of the group
  before, and a chord is placed whole [1]. A finger can rest on its string for the full gap [9].
- **Cost.** A change of direction costs more than a run [11]. A string that sounds again in the
  same hand costs much, because the finger damps it [12]. A damp costs about one more pluck time
  [12].
- **Pedals.** A new accidental of a note name is a move of the pedal of that note, by its foot
  (derived from [13]). Two moves of one foot need time between them [12].

### 7.10 Use in the app

`planHarpFingerings()` of `src/pose/harp-fingering.ts` plans the fingering of both hands, a pure
function of the notes. Design 5.3 "Harp" gives the motion. The app follows these facts:

- **The split of the hands (7.1, 7.9).** The right hand plays down to G2, and the left hand up to
  D4 (`HANDS_HOLDS.harp.reach`). The near harp of the short arms puts the higher strings near the
  chest of the deer, so the right hand takes them. One hand reaches a tenth and plays at most four strings
  (`span`, `most`).
- **The groups and the fingers (7.4, 7.9).** The notes of a hand go into groups of at most four
  strings within a tenth. The thumb plays the highest string of a group and finger 4 the lowest,
  so a scale plays 4321 up and 1234 down. A wide gap at the bottom of a group of two or three
  strings takes finger 3 or 4. The first note of a group that goes on with a run gets a crossing
  flag: finger 4 under the thumb up, or the thumb over finger 4 down (`HarpFingering.crossings`).
- **Placing (7.2).** The hand places each group right after the last pluck of the group before
  (`HarpFingering.places`), and stays still in the group: the palm stands at the middle of the
  strings of the group.
- **The pluck (7.3).** The hand pulls away from the strings only at the last pluck of a group.
  Each fingered note has a pluck time and a close time (`plucks`, `closes`), and
  `fingerClosesAt()` gives the close of the thumb and of fingers 2 to 4.
- **Rolled chords (7.4).** A chord of three or more strings of both hands rolls up, with the last
  pluck on the note start. An octave does not roll. The strings swing from the plucks of the roll,
  and the sound keeps one start.
- **Damping (7.6).** After a hand rest of more than a second, the palm opens flat at the place of
  its group with the fingers open, then the hand places the next group.
- **The pedals (7.7).** Each note needs the pedal of its string in its notch, and the pedal changes
  as early as the music lets it, after the last pluck in the old notch (`planPedals()` of
  `src/pose/harp-pedals.ts`). The left foot moves D, C, and B, and the right foot E, F, G, and A.
  The changes of one foot keep 0.5 s apart when the music lets them. The foot goes to the pedal,
  presses it into its notch, and goes back to its rest (`poseHarpFeet()`).
- **The heels (7.7).** The pedals stand on the floor, and the hoof presses a pedal with the heel
  on the floor: it turns up around its heel until the front of its sole is on the pedal, and
  higher over the pedal in the move to it. The pedals of the near harp lie under the knees of the
  deer, so for a pedal behind the rest place of the toe, the heel lifts (`HEEL_LIFT`).

The app does not follow these facts:

- **The damp (7.6).** The flat palm does not press on the strings. The treble strings of the
  near harp lie near the chest of the deer, and a press put the right paw into the chest.
- **The fingers (7.1).** The paw of the deer has one `fingers` bone, so all fingers close
  together. A digit pose for a paw with a bone for each finger reads the finger, the
  pluck time, and the close time of each note (`HarpFingering`) and closes each finger from its
  pluck to its close time.
- **Thirds of fingers, glissandos, and repeated notes (7.5).** The app plays a glissando as single
  plucks, and a fast repeat on one string with one finger.
- **The left palm (section 3).** The left palm stands far above the low pluck points of the bass
  strings (section 6, "The level hands").
- **The cost (7.9).** The groups come from a greedy rule. No search weighs the cost of a change of
  direction or of a repeated string.
- **The spelling (7.7).** The app plays each black key as the sharp of the natural below it, so no
  pedal goes into the flat notch, and the app does not spell a note as a flat or a sharp to save
  a pedal change. A harpist spells G sharp as A flat when G natural comes soon.
